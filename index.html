About this exhibit

How to drive it

  • Drag the 3D instrument to orbit, scroll to zoom. Click a label for its instrument-physics card. Keys 1–8 switch modes, Space blanks the beam, C toggles clarity, L labels, E electrons and signals, R resets the view, X enlarges the results (the button next to it opens them in a separate window, for a second screen), I acquires, N turns auto-narration on or off, [ and ] hide or show the explanation and the controls (or the ‹ › buttons; a tab at the screen edge brings them back).
  • Drag the image to move the stage and scroll it to change magnification. Focus, stigmate (X and Y) and center the aperture with the wobbler, as on a real SEM; or press Auto focus.
  • AI mode (brain button, or /): type commands such as "switch to BSE", "20 kV", "focus plus 0.5", "calibrate the magnification" or "reconstruct the relief". Near misses are corrected and confirmed. Voice: hold M or the microphone and speak the same commands (the browser's speech recognition; in Chrome and Edge on Google's or Microsoft's servers). With an AI agent connected (Controls → Voice and agent), questions get spoken answers.
  • Python (terminal button, or P): script the microscope with NumPy in the browser; the same script runs on a desktop, headless or driving this exhibit.
  • Training (button, or T): focus and stigmate, center the aperture, image an insulator without charging, set up an EBSD map. Each hides the answer; rate your result, then see the truth. With your consent the session is recorded (anonymous) and may be shared with the research dataset.
  • Control panel (V): an on-screen hand panel for the keyboard and mouse (Q/W focus, A/S magnification, Z/X probe current, H/L and K/J multifunction, Tab for stigmator / beam shift / aperture / tilt, O/P context, B wobbler, G auto focus, arrows for the stage), or plug in the TEM-view Console or any game controller.
  • Digital twin (bottom of the controls): an instrument profile (gun, lenses, scan, EDS detector), calibrated from data: magnification and scan distortion on the cross grating, gun brightness from the resolution, EDS resolution and energy scale. Practise on a mystery instrument; save sessions; export the data as NumPy .npz.
  • Cathodoluminescence (CL, key 7): the light the beam makes in GaN quantum wells, alumina or silica. Panchromatic or one wavelength (click the spectrum); dislocations show as dark spots. Change the voltage to reach the buried wells.
  • EBSD/TKD: maps are indexed point by point; hover a pixel to see its Kikuchi pattern, detected bands and orientation. EDS: click a peak to map it. BSE: click the quadrants to add, subtract or switch off each segment.

What is computed, and how

Images are formed pixel by pixel from multiscale specimen models (height, material, crystal orientation). SE yield follows the universal δ(E) curve and the secant law, with an edge effect scaled by the electron range and SE2 generated by backscattered electrons. BSE yield follows Reuter/Arnal η(Z, tilt), with channelling contrast from the local crystal orientation; each of the four quadrants records on its own. The ETD includes shadowing and grid bias. The probe diameter is the quadrature sum of source image, Cₛ, Cc, diffraction and an off-axis aperture, from the instrument profile; astigmatism makes the probe elliptical with two line foci; depth of field blurs out-of-focus heights. Charging appears for uncoated insulators above E2.

EBSD/TKD patterns are Kikuchi bands in gnomonic projection (width 2θB, strength from structure factors), mixed over the interaction footprint. They’re indexed with a real Hough transform and interplanar-angle voting, and compared with the true orientation. EDS uses Anderson–Hasler X-ray ranges, overvoltage, a simplified absorption correction, Kramers bremsstrahlung and a Fano-limited detector. Interaction volume is a single-scattering Monte Carlo (screened Rutherford, Joy–Luo stopping power). CL makes electron–hole pairs (one per ~3 Eg) across the generation volume, lets them diffuse to dislocations (dark) or into buried quantum wells, and emits each material’s bands, recorded through a parabolic mirror by a photomultiplier or a spectrometer.

Simplifications: pattern intensities are kinematic, matrix corrections are schematic, and the specimens are procedural. Trends, length scales and the metrology are right; absolute intensities are illustrative. The sibling exhibit for transmission microscopy is Seeing with Electrons (TEM-view).
